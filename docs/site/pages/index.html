Helisoundkoduhome

The name is Estonian: heli is sound, kodu is home. Say it HE-li-ko-du.

A home for the music you own. Helikodu is an offline audio player and music library manager for Linux. It plays practically every audio format, plays it correctly, keeps up with very large collections, and never connects to the internet.

{{cover formats}}

It plays practically everything

Helikodu plays practically every audio format there is on Linux, and the sound track of video files. It never shows video. Files are recognised by what is in them, not by their names, so a file with the wrong extension still plays.

Lossless
FLAC ALAC WAV AIFF WavPack APE TAK TTA Wave64 CAF
Lossy
MP3 AAC Vorbis Opus Musepack WMA AC-3 E-AC-3 DTS MP2 Speex AMR
DSD
DSF DFF Converted to high-resolution PCM, or sent to the DAC as DoP in exclusive mode.
Tracker modules
MOD S3M XM IT And more, through libopenmpt.
Game music
NSF SPC GBS VGM And more, through libgme. Looping tunes play their loop twice and fade out.
Commodore 64
SID Through libsidplayfp. With the song lengths of the High Voltage SID Collection, every tune plays for its real length.
MIDI
MID With a SoundFont you choose, or one your distribution installs.
Whole albums and books in one file
CUE M4B MKA WebM CUE sheets, separate, embedded or in a FLAC CUESHEET block, and chapters split a file into tracks of their own.
The sound of video files
MP4 MKV WebM AVI MOV Only the sound is decoded. No picture is ever shown.
One track for each song
Modules, game music files and SID tunes that hold several songs become one track per song in the library.
Chapters
Files with chapters play chapter by chapter, and the seek bar shows where each chapter starts.
More than one audio stream
A file with several audio streams can play another one.
Tags of every kind
ID3v2, ID3v1, APEv2, Vorbis comments, MP4 atoms, RIFF INFO and others, with several artists and genres per track. Text in old character sets, such as Windows-1251 or Shift-JIS, is repaired.
What this computer can play
Supported formats, opened from About, lists every family of formats, the decoder behind it and whether your build has it.
Two limits, said plainly
Game audio streams play only in builds made with vgmstream, which the ready-built programs leave out. No SoundFont is included.

Gapless
Tracks that share a format join sample-exactly. Tracks of one album stay gapless even when crossfade is on.
Exact seeking
A seek lands on the exact frame wherever the format allows it.
Crossfades and fades
Crossfade between tracks and when you skip, with equal-power gains, exact to the frame. Short fades for pause, stop, seek and skip, so nothing clicks.
Crossfades that follow the music
Every track is analysed once in the background. A crossfade then starts where the ending track fades out, never longer than the length you chose.
Silence, left out
If you like, the silence at the start and end of tracks is left out, and long silence inside a track, such as before a hidden track, is skipped.
A leveller
Tracks without ReplayGain values can be evened out as they play, gradually, by at most 12 dB.
A seek bar that shows the music
Its bars follow the loudness of the track. They are worked out in the background, never while the library is being scanned.
Lyrics
Synced and plain, from a file beside the track or from its tags. Each track's timing can be moved in steps of 0.1 s.
Play modes
Shuffle by tracks, albums, folders or groupings, or within each album. Repeat a track, an album or the queue. Stop after this track, this album or the queue. Play only this artist or this album.
Music that never stops
Continue with related tracks when the queue runs out, or let the Auto-DJ keep tracks from your library queued ahead. Start Radio begins from a track, an album or an artist.
Faster or slower
From half to twice the speed, for audiobooks, podcasts or practising along, with the pitch kept or following the speed.
Previous means previous
With shuffle on, Previous retraces the tracks in the order they actually played.
A queue with a memory
What played, what plays and what comes next. When Helikodu starts again, the queue, the position and the modes are as you left them, and music that was playing plays on.
Long tracks resume
Audiobooks and tracks over 20 minutes continue where you stopped, even after you played something else.
Settings of a track's own
Leave a track out of shuffle, play it only when you start it yourself, or give it its own volume.
Honest play counts
A track counts as played after half of it or four minutes, whichever comes first. Skipping ahead does not count as listening.
Slow drives
A track can be read into memory whole before it plays, for drives that are slow or go to sleep.
{{cover output}}

You choose where the sound goes

Pick the sound system, the device and the mode. Change any of them while music plays, and playback moves to the new device at the same position.

Four sound systems
PipeWire, PulseAudio, ALSA and JACK. Left on Automatic, Helikodu uses PipeWire when it is running and ALSA otherwise.
Shared or exclusive
In shared mode you hear other programs as well. In exclusive mode Helikodu takes an ALSA hardware device from PipeWire and gives it back when playback stops, or asks PipeWire for exclusive use at the track's own sample rate.
Bit-perfect
When nothing on the way changes the sound, the samples reach the device exactly as they are in the file: 16, 24 and 32 bits, the last without passing through floating point. The deck says when this is so.
The rate follows the music
The device opens at the track's own rate when it can, so a 44.1 kHz album and a 96 kHz album both play unconverted. When a rate has to be converted, you choose the quality of the resampler.
DSD to the DAC
DSF and DFF files go out as DoP when the device can take it, with DSD silence in pauses so the DAC stays in DSD mode. Otherwise they are converted to PCM.
It asks before it is loud
Before Helikodu first plays at full output level on a device, it asks. No sound reaches the device until you answer.
Devices are remembered
By a stable name, so a DAC is found again after a restart or in another USB port. If it disappears, playback pauses, moves to the default device or stops, as you choose.
Four ways to set the volume
The system's volume, Helikodu's own, the device's hardware mixer, or fixed at 100 % for amplifiers with a knob of their own.

Loudness, evened out
ReplayGain 2.0 and Opus R128 values even out albums mastered at different levels: by track, by album, or automatically, with the album's gain while an album plays in order and the track's otherwise.
It measures what has no values
Tracks and albums are measured following EBU R128: loudness, loudness range, sample peak and true peak. In the background, CUE tracks included, and written to the tags if you like.
Clipping, prevented your way
Lower the gain until the loudest peak fits, or keep the full gain and let the limiter catch the peaks.
A chain in your order
Pitch, equalizer, convolution, crossfeed, channel mix, compressor and limiter, in the order you drag them to. Keep an order and the stages that are on as a saved chain.
Dither where it is needed
Added by itself when processed sound goes to a device with fewer bits.
Always visible
A readout on the page, and the lamp on the deck, say whether the samples pass unchanged or are processed.

Scanned in separate processes
Tags are read by worker processes with time and memory limits, so a damaged file cannot take the player down.
It keeps itself up to date
New, changed, moved and deleted files show up within about a second. Network shares and FUSE mounts, which cannot be watched, are scanned again every 30 minutes.
Drives that come and go
Tracks on a drive that is not connected are hidden, not forgotten. When the drive is back, they return with their ratings and play counts.
Your statistics survive a tidy-up
Play counts, skips, ratings, resume positions and places in playlists stay with a track when its file is moved or renamed.
Albums as they are
Albums of several discs, compilations, several artists and genres per track, credits by role, sort names and MusicBrainz identifiers are all understood.
Covers from your own files
Pictures inside the files and image files in the album folder, stored once and cached as thumbnails. Nothing is downloaded.
Backups
The library is backed up once a day and the last seven backups are kept. A damaged database is replaced by the newest good backup at the next start.
Labels of your own
Give tracks, albums and artists words of your own, such as late night or to sort, browse by them and search for them. They stay in the library, never in your files.
Bring your history along
Play counts, ratings and playlists come over from Rhythmbox, Strawberry, Clementine, Lollypop and iTunes, with a preview first and an undo afterwards. Your own data moves to another computer in one file.
A managed folder, if you want one
Helikodu can also copy or move music you add into one folder and name it for you, and gather the rest of the library there. Each file is checked before an original goes.
You say what is scanned
Patterns to skip, .nomedia files and hidden folders are respected. The sound of video files joins the library only if you ask for it.

Search that understands questions

Type a word and the results appear while you type. Write a field and an operator, and the same box becomes a query language. That language also builds smart playlists, filters the track table and tells a sync what to copy.

Type thisTo find
genre:jazz -genre:fusionJazz, but no fusion
artist:kask year:2018..2021One artist, within a range of years
rating>=4 -genre:ambientFour stars and more, without ambient
added<30d sort:added descWhat was added in the last 30 days, the newest first
title~"^Intro" OR duration<30sTitles that match a regular expression, or anything shorter than half a minute
is:lossless has:lyrics limit:50The first 50 lossless tracks that have lyrics
tag:mood=calmAny other tag that is in your files
plays=0What you have never played

Smart playlists
All or any of the rules, groups of rules, a limit by tracks, minutes, hours or gigabytes, and an order of their own.
Generator playlists
Steps that each work on what the step before left: from the library, a playlist or a search, then filters, sorts, at most so many per artist or album, and a limit.
Favourites
The heart on the deck adds the playing track to Favourites, always the first playlist.
Undo for every change
Every change of a playlist can be undone and done again with Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z. Only deleting a playlist asks first.
Folders and tidying
Playlists go into folders. One command removes duplicates, another the missing tracks, a third shuffles the order for good.
In and out
Import and export M3U, M3U8, PLS and XSPF. Playlist files in your library folders can become playlists that follow their files.
Ratings
One to five stars, kept in the library and safe across moves. Ratings that other players wrote into the tags are taken over, and Helikodu writes its own into the files if you turn that on.
Your history is yours

{{cover offline}}

Offline, by construction

Helikodu contains no network code at all. That is a deliberate rule, not a missing feature, and it will not change. It starts and works the same with or without a network, it never waits for a server, and nothing about your listening leaves your computer.

What that rules out

  • Internet radio and streaming services
  • Looking up covers, artist photos or lyrics online
  • Scrobbling
  • Checking for updates
  • Telemetry, accounts and crash reporting

How the rule is kept

  • No network, TLS or DNS library may enter the build: the dependency check rejects them.
  • The decoder opens local files only, and refuses to open anything a file points to.
  • Every release is tested where there is no network, and the tests fail if anything tries to open an internet connection.
  • A playlist entry with a web address is shown as unavailable and never fetched.

The only connections Helikodu makes are local ones on your computer: D-Bus, for media keys and desktop integration, and the sound server. In the Flatpak, the sandbox enforces it as well: Helikodu has no network permission there (see what the Flatpak may do).

What it knows about you
Only what is on your computer, and it keeps it there. Your library, play history and settings are in your home folder. Logs are off by default, and a crash report is written to a file and never sent.
Your files change only when you ask
When you save tags, write loudness values, organize files or move copies to the trash. Everything else Helikodu learns stays in its own database.
Careful with broken files
Every parser is fuzzed. A file that fails is skipped, and a track that made the player crash twice is passed over until you play it yourself.

Install with Flatpak opens your software centre, which installs Helikodu from Helikodu's own Flatpak repository and keeps it up to date from there, like any other application. From version 0.9.3 on, the Flatpak file from GitHub adds the same repository when you install it.

Every file except the Arch package also comes for 64-bit ARM computers (aarch64, or arm64 for the Debian and Ubuntu packages). The Flatpak and the AppImage bring their own libraries; the packages use those of their distribution. The ready-built archives were built on Debian 13 and need FFmpeg 7.1; where only a newer FFmpeg is installed, use the Flatpak or the AppImage instead. Installing, step by step says which file suits which system.

What the Flatpak may do, and why

In the Flatpak, Helikodu has no network access at all and asks only for what its features need. Software centres list these permissions and may rate them as potentially unsafe, as they do for most music players that manage files. Helikodu shows the same list the first time it starts, and under About ▸ Permissions.

  • Your Music folder, to read and change it: for the library, the tag editor, organizing files and cover files. Other folders are added in the settings.
  • The sound system (PipeWire and PulseAudio), to play and to choose the output device.
  • Reserving a sound card, so that the sound server hands it over for exclusive, bit-perfect output.
  • The login manager (logind), to pause playback before the computer goes to sleep or the screen locks.
  • The system tray, for the optional tray icon.
